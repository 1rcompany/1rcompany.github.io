Projects

Credit Risk Analysis and Corporate Financial Distress Prediction

Course project · Data Analysis and Machine Learning with Python · Grade A

Built a company-month pipeline using over one million CRSP firm-month observations from 2016–2025 to predict financial distress proxy events within the next 12 months. Engineered market-based features covering momentum, volatility, downside risk, liquidity, trading volume, price level, and firm size.

Compared logistic regression and random forest models. The random forest achieved a test ROC-AUC of 0.895; the highest-risk decile had a 14.0% event rate.

Relative Pricing of Islamic and Conventional Fixed-Income Securities: Evidence from Malaysian Sovereign Sukuk

Term paper · Intro to Finance Research · Grade A+
Supervisor: Professor Yao-Min Chiang
Being developed into an undergraduate honors thesis.

Examined yield differentials between Malaysian Government Investment Issues and Malaysian Government Securities across benchmark tenors. Constructed a monthly panel for 2014–2024 to study liquidity frictions, supply scarcity, primary-market demand, and Islamic investor demand.

Estimated panel OLS models with tenor and year fixed effects, Newey-West HAC standard errors, robustness checks, VIF diagnostics, and residual analysis.

Confidence-Based OOD Detection and Risk-Aware Prediction in Image Classification

Final project · Intro to ML and Deep Learning, Department of Civil Engineering · Grade A

Trained a ResNet18 classifier on CIFAR-10 and evaluated distribution-shift detection using SVHN, CIFAR-100, Gaussian noise, and rotated CIFAR images.

Compared maximum softmax confidence, logit margin, and negative energy using AUROC, FPR@95TPR, and AUPR. Calibration and risk-coverage analysis showed why accuracy alone is insufficient for reliable prediction.
